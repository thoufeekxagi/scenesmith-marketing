Yes. To schedule LinkedIn posts without paying for a separate scheduler, use the clock in LinkedIn’s post composer. For a personal profile, LinkedIn currently allows a post from 10 minutes to three months ahead. For a LinkedIn Page, the minimum is one hour; scheduling requires a Page super admin or content admin. Both queues are built into LinkedIn.

This native option is enough if you only need to queue a LinkedIn post. You do not need a separate paid scheduler for that workflow.

## How to schedule posts on LinkedIn

On desktop, open LinkedIn and choose **Start a post**. On mobile, tap **Post**. Confirm whether you are publishing as your personal profile or a LinkedIn Page, then add the text and media you want to publish.

For a **personal profile**:

1. Select the **clock** icon in the composer.
2. Choose a date and time. LinkedIn currently limits the scheduled time to between 10 minutes and three months from now.
3. Select **Next** and check the scheduled date and time displayed above your draft.
4. Select **Schedule** and wait for LinkedIn’s confirmation.

For a **LinkedIn Page**, open the Page’s post composer, choose the clock, pick a date and time at least one hour ahead, review the schedule, and select **Schedule**. You need to be a Page super admin or content admin. The confirmation is the useful final check: it tells you that LinkedIn accepted the schedule. A draft sitting in the composer is not scheduled yet.

## Check the time zone before scheduling

LinkedIn says profile-post times are converted to UTC using your device’s time-zone setting. If you are planning a post while travelling, using a shared computer, or scheduling for a team in another region, confirm the time shown in the final composer before you select **Schedule**. Avoid relying on a time written in a planning document without checking how LinkedIn displays it on the device you are using.

## Find, edit, or cancel a scheduled post

To review the queue, open the composer for the same profile or Page and select the clock icon. Choose **View all scheduled posts** on desktop or **View all** on mobile. LinkedIn’s scheduled-post view lets you preview a post, change its schedule, edit it, or delete it. Page admins can also review and manage posts scheduled by other Page admins.

Make one final pass over the scheduled item: check the account or Page you are posting as, the opening lines, attached media, links, date, and time. If you edit the post after reviewing it, review the saved version again before leaving the queue.

## What the free LinkedIn scheduler does not cover

The limits differ by destination. LinkedIn’s profile help lists **Events and Jobs** as unsupported post types. Its Page help also excludes **multiple photos, reshares, polls, and Services**. A Page post must be scheduled at least one hour ahead; a profile post can be scheduled 10 minutes ahead. Both can be scheduled up to three months in advance. If the clock option is missing or LinkedIn rejects the post, check the posting identity and format first. The native scheduler is also a LinkedIn-only queue; it does not coordinate your Instagram and X posts or provide one cross-channel calendar.

Here is a simple weekly plan you can adapt. The days and times are examples, not claims about a universal best time to post:

- **Monday:** answer one question customers asked you this week.
- **Wednesday:** explain a useful process or show an example using information you can verify.
- **Friday:** share one lesson, update, or thoughtful question related to the week’s work.

Write the source note before drafting, schedule only posts you are ready to stand behind, and use your own audience data to adjust the timing. Scheduling helps you keep a plan; it does not guarantee reach or engagement.

## Questions about free LinkedIn scheduling

### Can you schedule LinkedIn posts without paying for a scheduler?

Yes. LinkedIn’s built-in composer has a scheduling control, so a separate scheduling subscription is not needed for a basic LinkedIn post.

### How far ahead can I schedule a LinkedIn post?

For a personal profile, LinkedIn currently allows 10 minutes to three months ahead. For a Page, it is one hour to three months. Check the composer because platform limits can change.

### Can I change a post after scheduling it?

Yes. Open **View all scheduled posts**, select the post, and use the available edit, reschedule, or delete controls.

### Where can I see examples of LinkedIn posts to schedule?

Start from a real source note: a customer question you are allowed to discuss, a product change that has shipped, or a process your team can explain.
