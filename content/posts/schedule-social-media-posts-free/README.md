Yes, and you probably already own the tool. LinkedIn builds scheduling into its own post composer, and Meta Business Suite is Meta's scheduler for Instagram and Facebook — Meta's own product page describes it as "a free tool" and says you can schedule posts and stories there for both apps without switching accounts. Neither one needs a third-party subscription.

What is not free is the part you may actually be missing: a draft written for you, one queue across both platforms, and an approval step before anything goes out. That last mile is where an AI connector earns its place, and this article marks the exact point where the free route stops.

## What "free" covers on each platform

- **LinkedIn, personal posts.** LinkedIn's help article *Schedule posts* (page header: last updated about a week before 16 October 2026, read 2026-10-16) puts scheduling in the regular composer: click **Start a post**, write it, click the **Clock icon** in the lower-right corner, pick the date and time, and confirm. Your scheduled posts all sit in one place, and the Clock icon carries a badge showing how many are queued.
- **LinkedIn, Page posts.** A Page's super admin or content admin can schedule a post or a newsletter article from an hour ahead to three months ahead, per LinkedIn's *Scheduled posts for LinkedIn Pages* page (read 2026-10-16).
- **Instagram and Facebook.** Meta Business Suite creates and schedules posts for both destinations, and its Planner shows "a calendar view of your Facebook Page and/or Instagram profile's posts, stories, reels and ads" (*Use Planner in Meta Business Suite on desktop*, read 2026-10-16).
- **What none of it does.** Writing the post, checking that the draft reads the way you want, and keeping one calendar across two platforms. Each composer schedules for its own platform, and you type every draft by hand.

If you only need "post this at 9am without me being there", the native tools are enough and this is the whole answer. The rest of the article is the setup and the checks that stop a free schedule from failing quietly.

## Step 1 — Check who is allowed to schedule, and where the post lands

![A deep teal rounded padlock with a vivid orange arch for a shackle on a cream background.](/blog/schedule-social-media-posts-free-step-01.webp)

Two permission facts decide whether the buttons you are about to use will work:

- **Instagram or a Facebook Page in Meta Business Suite:** Meta requires either full control of the Page or Instagram account, or **Content** permission on it (*Create and manage posts in Meta Business Suite on desktop*, read 2026-10-16). A colleague with "Ads" access only can see the account and still not schedule an organic post.
- **A LinkedIn Page:** scheduling is available to a **super admin or content admin** of that Page. On a personal profile you schedule your own posts, and there is no role question to settle first.
- **The Instagram account type:** publishing tools reach **Instagram professional accounts — business and creator**. Meta's own platform overview (header: *Updated Sep 28, 2026*, read 2026-10-16) says the Instagram Platform "allows your app to access data for Instagram professional accounts, including business and creator accounts". A personal profile is not served at all.

That third check is the one people skip, and it is also the reason the Facebook Page question keeps coming up: Meta runs **two** Instagram API configurations, and only the Facebook-Login one requires the account to be linked to a Facebook Page. The Instagram-Login configuration "serves Instagram professional accounts without requiring a linked Facebook Page". Native scheduling inside Instagram and Business Suite is a different path from those APIs, but the account-type requirement is the same: professional account, no personal profiles.

## Step 2 — Schedule a LinkedIn post from the composer

![A deep teal block with a square notch cut out of its lower right, overlapped by a large vivid orange circle with a small orange square above it on a cream background.](/blog/schedule-social-media-posts-free-step-02.webp)

1. On LinkedIn, click **Start a post** at the top of your homepage (or, for a Page, open the Page and start your post as its admin).
2. Write the post text. Add the media you want — the schedule step happens after the content, not before it.
3. Click the **Clock icon** in the lower-right corner. A *Schedule post* window opens.
4. Choose the date and time, confirm, and the post joins your queue. LinkedIn's window for a personal post is **10 minutes to 3 months** from the current time; on a Page it is **1 hour to 3 months**.
5. Know what cannot be scheduled: LinkedIn's help page lists **Events** and **Jobs** posts as unsupported for scheduling.

The three-month ceiling is worth remembering before you plan a quarter of content in one sitting — anything past it has to wait, or come from a tool that holds the draft itself.

## Step 3 — Schedule Instagram (and Facebook) in Meta Business Suite

![Two rounded squares side by side, one deep teal and one vivid orange, each with a cream dot, joined by a vivid orange bar across the top on a cream background.](/blog/schedule-social-media-posts-free-step-03.webp)

Meta Business Suite gives you two doors to the same queue.

**Create post, then schedule it.**

1. Open Meta Business Suite, then click **Create post** from the home page or the **Content** tab.
2. Under **Post to**, pick the destination — your Facebook Page, your Instagram profile, or both where the format allows.
3. Add the media and the text. Meta notes that the limit for posting to Instagram is **10 photos** in one post.
4. Under **Scheduling options**, set the date and time. There is a shortcut worth using: **Active times** suggests slots based on when your followers were most active over the past 7 days.
5. Click **Schedule**. The other two buttons are **Publish** (immediately) and **Finish later** (a draft).

**Or plan it on the calendar.** In **Planner**, hover an upcoming day, click **Schedule**, and choose what you are scheduling — for example **Schedule post**. Planner then shows published and scheduled content in a weekly or monthly view, for the Page, the Instagram account, or both together.

Meta's own product page for Business Suite says you can "schedule your posts and stories" for Facebook and Instagram from one place, so Stories go through the same scheduling step, not a separate tool.

## Step 4 — Verify the queue, because a scheduled post is not a published post

![A deep teal tray holding a row of vivid orange cards with a large cream check mark on the card facing the viewer, on a cream background.](/blog/schedule-social-media-posts-free-step-04.webp)

The failure mode of native scheduling is silence: the post is queued, the time arrives, and something about permissions, the destination or the account type means nothing publishes. Two checks catch nearly all of it.

- **LinkedIn:** the **Clock icon** shows a badge with the number of scheduled posts. Open it to see the queue, and use **Modify schedule** or **Edit post** on a queued item to change it.
- **Meta Business Suite:** open the **Content** tab and select the **Scheduled** tab (the others are Published, Drafts, Expiring and Expired). From a post you can **Edit post**, **Reschedule post**, **Move to drafts** or **Delete post**.
- **Check the destination, not just the time.** If you manage several accounts, confirm the scheduled post is going to the handle you meant. On Meta, the composer's **Post to** selection is the destination; on LinkedIn, a Page post and a personal post are separate queues on separate surfaces.
- **Check the time zone.** Meta states that scheduling times correspond to your current time zone, so a queue built while travelling can shift against your audience.

Do the check on the day you schedule, and again on the day it should publish. Reading the live post is the only proof.

## Questions readers ask

### Is scheduling a post free on LinkedIn?

Yes. LinkedIn's help article for scheduling posts places the feature in the standard composer — **Start a post**, then the Clock icon — with no plan requirement mentioned, and scheduled posts live in one queue you can open from that icon. LinkedIn's documentation for Pages frames the same feature as a Page admin capability rather than a paid add-on: a super admin or content admin schedules between an hour and three months ahead.

### Can you schedule Instagram Stories for free?

Meta says yes, from its own tools. Meta Business Suite's product page states that you can "schedule your posts and stories" for Facebook and Instagram in one place, and the Planner's calendar covers "posts, stories, reels and ads". Scheduled Stories appear in the same Scheduled view as your other content. One caveat for anyone publishing through an API-based tool instead: SceneSmith's Instagram publishing covers single-image posts, Reels and multi-image carousels, so a Story schedule belongs in Meta's own tools.

### Do you need a Business or Creator account to schedule Instagram posts?

For anything that publishes through Meta's Instagram Platform — including connector tools — the account has to be an **Instagram professional account**, business or creator; Meta's platform overview describes the platform that way, and a personal profile is not served by the publishing endpoints. For plain native scheduling inside Instagram and Meta Business Suite, the professional-account setup is what unlocks the scheduling and insights surfaces you are scheduling from. Converting the account is done on Instagram's side, in the app's account settings.

### Can ChatGPT schedule posts for me?

Only through a connector, and only with an approval step. A hosted MCP server can expose a scheduling tool to ChatGPT or Codex, so the time you specify in the conversation becomes a queued post — but the server holds the account access and returns a draft for you to approve, because the assistant itself has no Instagram or LinkedIn account. The [ChatGPT-to-Instagram guide](/blog/link-chatgpt-to-instagram) and the [connector guide](/blog/chatgpt-connector-to-linkedin) walk through both destinations, and [which ChatGPT plan and surface you need](/blog/chatgpt-mcp-support) is covered separately.

### Why did my scheduled post not publish at the right time?

Three causes cover most cases. First, permissions: Meta requires full control or **Content** permission on the Page or Instagram account, and a LinkedIn Page needs a super admin or content admin. Second, the destination: a queue built for a Page or one Instagram handle does not publish to another. Third, the time zone: Meta states that scheduling times follow your current time zone, so a queue set in one zone can land hours away from the audience you had in mind. Re-check the queued item in LinkedIn's Clock menu or in Meta Business Suite's **Content → Scheduled** tab before you assume the post was lost.
